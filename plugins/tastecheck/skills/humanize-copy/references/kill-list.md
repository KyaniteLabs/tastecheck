# Humanize Copy — The 2026 Kill List (with alternatives & rewrites)

The full vocabulary and phrase blacklist, by category, with human replacements and
worked before/after rewrites. Read when editing real copy. The rule is never "find a
synonym" — it's "replace the buzzword with a concrete specific."

## Why these words are tells
Models trained on millions of mediocre corporate documents default to the average of
that corpus: high-syllable, low-meaning jargon that *sounds* professional and *says*
nothing. Since LLMs went mainstream, the frequency of these words in business writing
spiked, so readers (and detectors, and Google's helpful-content signals) now use them
as automated-junk markers.

## Vocabulary blacklist → human alternative

| Category | AI red-flag words | Human alternatives |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Intro/verbs | delve, dive into, demystify, navigate, uncover, unpack | look at, explore, go through, now, basically |
| Buzzword verbs | leverage, empower, foster, streamline, optimize, elevate, ignite, unleash, unlock, underscore | use, help, give you, cut, build, start, access, show |
| Buzzword adjectives | seamless, robust, cutting-edge, future-ready, scalable, dynamic, comprehensive | reliable, strong, new, full — or *name the actual feature* |
| High-drama | transformative, revolutionary, game-changer, paradigm shift | impactful, major, helpful, big change |
| Vague adjectives | myriad, plethora, pivotal, unwavering, multifaceted | many, lots of, key, steady, complex |
| Spatial metaphors | landscape, tapestry, realm, beacon, journey, roadmap, symphony, ecosystem | situation, mix, guide, plan, process, blend |
| Transitions | furthermore, moreover, additionally, in conclusion, it is important to note | also, plus, besides, to wrap up, keep in mind |
| Drama nouns | testament, synergy | proof, working together |

"Seamless" deserves special mention: in software it's the single most diluted word —
it's a placeholder for a missing description. Say what actually happens instead.

## Phrase tells → fix

| AI phrase | Why it's a tell | Fix |
|-----------|-----------------|-----|
| "In today's fast-paced / ever-evolving world/landscape" | token-buying filler | delete; start with the point |
| "Let's dive in" / "Let's explore" | lazy-prompt opener | delete |
| "It's not just X, it's Y" / "Not only… but also…" | the parallelism tic | state what it IS, directly |
| "In conclusion / At the end of the day / Ultimately / In essence" | essay throat-clearing | end on a point or CTA |
| "Here's the kicker / That's only half the story / Real talk / Here's the truth" | fake hooks | link ideas by their logic |
| "Picture this / Imagine a world where / As a [role], you know" | faked empathy | a specific grainy detail |
| "This article aims to explore…" | unsure framing | "This shows you…" / just do it |
| "It's important to note / It could be argued / Generally speaking" | hedging | cut; commit to the claim |

## Before / after rewrites

**Buzzword stack**
- ✗ "We empower users to leverage a comprehensive ecosystem to optimize their
  workflows and foster innovation."
- ✓ "We give you one place to run your work, so your team moves faster."

**Spatial metaphor**
- ✗ "A beacon in the evolving landscape of data security."
- ✓ "Security that catches problems before your users do."

**The parallelism**
- ✗ "It's not just a CRM, it's a growth engine."
- ✓ "It's a CRM that tells you which deals are about to stall."

**Filler opener**
- ✗ "In today's fast-paced digital world, businesses need to stay ahead."
- ✓ "Your competitors ship weekly. You ship quarterly. That's the problem."

**Hedge → stance**
- ✗ "In this article, we will discuss the importance of reducing churn."
- ✓ "If you don't fix churn by Q3, your ad budget is at risk."

**Fake experience → specific**
- ✗ "Imagine a busy restaurant during the dinner rush."
- ✓ "When the POS dies at 7 PM Friday and you're two servers short…"

## The kill-list audit (process)
1. Search the draft for every word/phrase above.
2. For each hit, ask: *what specific thing am I actually trying to say?* Replace the
   buzzword with that specific (a number, a named feature, a concrete noun).
3. Read the result aloud (pub test). If you wouldn't say it to a colleague, redo it.
4. Don't swap a buzzword for a fancier word — that's the over-correction tell. Plainer
   is more human.

## A note on detectors and "bypassing"
The goal here is **good writing**, not gaming a detector. The same moves — specifics,
varied rhythm, a real stance, verified facts — that make copy read as human are what
make it *better* (more trusted, more useful, more rankable). Humanizer tools that
scramble syntax create new tells; manual editing with real voice is what works.
